# What the earlier layers said, district by district (for the revised-gazetteer comparison)

Sources: `../../results/README.md` (Bombay), `../../madras/README.md`, `../../north/README.md`,
`../../bengal/README.md`, `../../punjab/README.md`, `../../cp/README.md`, `../../census-images/README.md`.
Coded rows with quotes are in each folder's `results/coded/`.

## Bombay Presidency → Maharashtra / Mysore (Karnataka)

- **Dharwar** (1884):
  - Marāṭhās (44,085), Komṭis (8,238) and Khatrīs (4,060) have the Śaṅkarācārya as guru.
  - The Komṭis' Śaṅkarācārya acts through a deputy, Bhāskarācārya.
  - The Deshasthas are split between Smārtas and Mādhvas; Konkaṇasthas look to Saṅkeśvar.
  - About 7.6% of Hindus are in castes asserted Smārta.
- **Bijapur** (1884):
  - Marāṭhās (15,877) are "Smārts … their guide being Shankarāchārya".
  - The Smārta Deshasthas' guide is at Sonda.
- **Kolhapur** (1886): the Deshasthas' "high priest is Shankaracharya … who lives at Sankeshvar". No Karvīr
  Śaṅkarācārya is named. The Saṅkeśvar Svāmi's maṭhs stand in Kolhapur city.
- **Poona** (1885):
  - The Brahmans' pontiff is the head of Śṛṅgeri.
  - The Kuṇbīs "cannot tell whether they are Smarts or Bhāgvats".
- **Ahmadnagar** (1884): Deshasthas split between Smārtas and Bhāgavatas; "the decrees of Shankaracharya at
  Shankeshvar" are final; Kuṇbīs "cannot tell".
- **Ratnagiri** (1880): the 1872 census returned 99.7% of Hindus as "Śaiva". The Smārta Shenvis look to a
  svāmi at Sonavda (Kavale).
- **Census 1881:** 33,851 Gosāvīs in the presidency, 19,247 of them in the Deccan.

## Madras Presidency → Madras/Tamil Nadu, Mysore/Karnataka

- **South Kanara** (Thurston 1909):
  - Śṛṅgeri is guru seat for non-Brahmin castes (Kharvis: "disciples of the Sringeri mutt"; Marāṭhās,
    Servegāras, Ares, Chaptegāras, whose fines are paid to the Sringeri math).
  - Tulu Brahmans also look to Śṛṅgeri, and the Haviks to Rāmacandrapura.
- **Madurai** (Tamil division, 1891 census):
  - Smārta sub-castes are 42% of Brahmans and Aiyaṅgārs 26%.
  - Thurston names no Śaṅkara seat for Tamil Smārtas.
  - Madura gazetteer (1906): "Brahman Smarta math" items are few.

## United Provinces → Uttar Pradesh / Haryana

- **Gonda:**
  - Goshains are "followers of Shankaracharya" in ten classes, with a Bhāratī mahant at Itara and a Ban
    mahant at Srinagar.
  - Goshains had 89 villages in 1869 and 34 in 1905; they monopolised the hill trade.
  - Census 1891: 11,478 Daśanāmīs.
- **Gorakhpur:**
  - Mahant Ram Ratangir of Harpur held 36 villages.
  - There were 13,944 Atīths, and 7,010 Daśanāmīs in 1891.
  - The Gorakhnāth (Nāth) temple stands here.
- **Faizabad:** 1,847 Atīts in 1901 and 5,371 Daśanāmīs in 1891. Ayodhyā is dominated by the Bairāgī akhāṛās.
- **Meerut:** 5,326 Gosāins in 1872 and 5,083 Daśanāmīs in 1891; Goshain clans held villages.
- **Mirzapur:**
  - Mahant Anand Gir held 39 villages, and Gosāins owned 44,784 acres.
  - Gosaintola was a quarter of Gosāin merchants; "Sanyasi" merchants traded with the Deccan.
  - Census 1891: 4,258 Daśanāmīs.
- **Varanasi:**
  - Daśanāmī Nāgā akhāṛās (Nirañjanī, Jūnā, "Nirbani") stand at Shivala and Hanuman ghats, with a "Dandi
    satra".
  - Gosāins held 10,304 acres; Gosain Narayangir is named.
- **Allahabad:**
  - The Nirbānī, Nirañjanī, Jūnā and Mahānirbānī akhāṛās stand at Daraganj, and the Magh/Kumbh melā is held
    here.
  - Gosāin land is 1.2% of the district; Mahant Partabgir was a banker.
- **Saharanpur:** the Kankhal Gosāins (Bhola Gir, 4,024 acres), the Nirañjanī and Nirbānī akhāṛās, and the
  1760 battle at Hardwar.
- **Moradabad:** the Salempur Gosāin math (Ban mahants, 36 villages), and Kundan Gir at Kundarkhi.
- **Bareilly:** 6,169 Gosāins in 1872 and 4,023 Daśanāmīs in 1891.
- **Almora:** the Pinnath Gosāin colony (copper-plate grants) and Aijnath (Baijnath) Gosāin tombs; Kumaon had
  8,860 Gosāins in 1872.
- **Azamgarh:** 2,186 Gosāins and 3,456 Atīts in 1881, but none in 1891 (5,764 "Jogis").
- **Sitapur:** 4,040 Goshains in 1869 and 4,414 Daśanāmīs in 1891.
- **Hissar** (Punjab):
  - 1,479 Gusains in 1881 and 2,318 Gosains in 1891.
  - The Puri guru is at Kharak and the Giri guru at Bilak.
  - Gosāins are split into gharbārī (married) and matdhārī (celibate, keeping maths).
- **Karnal** (Punjab): Gosain pujaris of Shiva temples, a Gir Gosain math said to be 800 years old, and the
  Ramthali Puri mahants (Rose).

## Bengal Presidency → Bihar / West Bengal / Orissa

- **Saran:**
  - 18,612 Atīths in 1872 and 26,753 in 1901.
  - Gharbārī Atīths were zamindars, moneylenders and cultivators; "followers of Sankaracharya".
- **Champaran:** 6,063 Atīths in 1872 and 9,768 in 1901.
- **Shahabad:** 3,454 Atīths in 1872 and 7,246 in 1901.
- **Gaya (Bodh Gayā):**
  - A Giri maṭh "established by Sankara Acharya" (16th century), with the farman of Taradih (c. 1727).
  - The Mahabodhi Society dispute: the High Court found the Mahant in possession (1906).
- **Monghyr:** "The mendicant or Sannyasi classes have acquired wealth": the Mahants of Dularpur and Suja.
- **Bhagalpur:** the Sultanganj convent of Sannyāsīs and its line of mahants (Harinath; Digambar, 13th).
- **Palamau:** 578 + 485 Atīths in 1901.
- **Hooghly (Tārakeśvar):** the mahant is "a celibate of the Dasnami order", with estates of Rs 16,000 a year.
- **Howrah (Bhot-Bagān):**
  - The Tashi Lama's grant to Puran Gir Gosāin (1785).
  - The mahant was elected in 1905 "by the other Dasnami Mahants of Bengal".
- **Puri:**
  - Hunter (1877) is silent on Śaṅkara's maṭh.
  - O'Malley (1908) has "the Sankaracharya" maṭh near Svargadvāra, with a library of about 1,200 manuscripts,
    but never the name Govardhana.
  - The Vaiṣṇava maṭhs (Emār and others) dominate.

## Central Provinces → Madhya Pradesh / Maharashtra

- **Raipur:**
  - The Dharampuri estate: from Bichipuri's akhāṛā (500 Nāgās for the Nagpur Raja) to a married mahant with
    40 villages by 1909.
  - The Deopur Nāgā Gosāins brought Danteshwari from Bastar.
  - Census 1891: 3,194 Gosāins.
- **Jabalpur:** Bheraghat, granted by Raghuji Bhonsla to Mahant Hardeo Puri Gosain and held by Jagjiwanpuri;
  2,080 Gosāins in 1891.
- **Nagpur:** Gosāins held 50–70 villages; 6,203 Gosāins in the 1860s and 2,914 in 1891 (Giri 1,299).
- **Akola / Amravati:** village priests are "more frequently Gosains than Brahmans", recognising the Mahant of
  Māhūr; Gosāvīs travelled in naked bands.
