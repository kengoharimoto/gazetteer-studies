# Census sources for the Daśanāmīs, NWP & Oudh / United Provinces

Files in this folder (IA OCR): `india.history.resource.117715` / `wbsl.4454` (1891 Imperial Caste Tables, Part
III), `india.history.resource.110233` / `wbsl.4279` (1891 Report and Provincial Tables, Part I),
`in.ernet.dli.2015.62746` (1901 vol. XVI Part II tables). The OCR of the 1891 and 1901 *tables* is scrambled
(only the caste index is legible), so district headcounts cannot be read from them.

## 1891 Report (Census of India 1891, vol. XVI, NWP & Oudh, ch. VII "Hinduism and its sects", ¶201)

- Saivite ascetic orders, from the sub-caste column:
  - **Goshain Dasnami 103,320; Goshain unspecified 38,477.** (The report's OCR reads "108,320"; the page images of Table XVI give 55,347 + 47,973 = 103,320. See `../../census-images/`.)
  - Jogi 78,387 (confirmed from the page images: 42,565 + 35,822).
  - Sanniyasi 4,406 and Udasi 2,809, separate sub-divisions of Fakir on p. 53 (n94); with Goshain Dasnami the
    Daśanāmīs were 107,726 (`../../census-images/results/up1891_ascetics.tsv`).
  - From the sect column, as printed: Aghori 1,006; Aughar 5,339; Alakhnami 10,886; Gorakhpanthi 28,816;
    minor Saivite sects 1,870.
- "The Dasnami Goshains contribute the best instructed and most respectable body of Saivite ascetics. They are
  recruited from the twice born castes …"
- It follows Wilson on the orders:
  - 3½ of the ten orders are "Dandis"; the remaining 6½ are "usually denominated Atits".
  - The Atīts "mix freely in the business of the world; they carry on trade and often accumulate property …
    Some of them even marry, but in that case they are distinguished by the name Sanjogi".
  - "Goshains of the latter six and a half classes are most numerous in these provinces."
- On the reliability of the figures (¶ on Fakir): "The words Goshain Bairagi, Jogi, Saniyasi, were apparently
  used by a considerable proportion of the enumerators as interchangeable, each appearing as a subdivision of
  the others." Census ascetic counts are therefore soft.
- Vallabhācārya "Goshains" (lineage heads of a Vaiṣṇava sect) are discussed separately. The same word covers
  both.
