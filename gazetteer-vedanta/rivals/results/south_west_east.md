# Rivals in the south, west and east (assignment S)

> **The study's verdict** (`../../SYNTHESIS.md`, "The answer"; `../README.md`): Śaṅkara's

## What the texts show (with figures)

### Gujarat: Vallabha and Svāminārāyaṇ

- **Totals (1901 summary, partly from the 1872 census):**
  - Vallabhācārīs, about 647,256: "the largest and most influential Vaishnav sect in Gujarat, Cutch, and Kathiawar"
    [S004].
  - Svāminārāyaṇs, 287,687 [S001].
  - The Śrī sampradāya, 1,156,848 in 1872, a figure that evidently includes Rāmānandī-type Vaiṣṇavas [S006].
  - Mādhvas: "very few" [S007]. Cambay nevertheless returned 712 in 1872 [S027].
- **Svāminārāyaṇ is the most institutional of the Gujarat sects.**
  - It has two territorial gādīs, at Ahmedabad and Vadtal. Lay followers owe a twentieth to a tenth of their
    yearly income [S002].
  - It has an order of Brahmacārīs, Sādhus and low-caste Pāḷās [S003].
  - Vadtal: a temple of 1824 costing Rs 75,000; 500–1000 inmates; eleven branch churches; two festivals of about
    20,000 pilgrims each [S009–S010].
  - Temples at Ahmedabad (1850), Surat (1869), Idar (Rs 50,000), Gadhda and Muli [S011, S016, S019–S020, S023].
- **Vallabha wealth sits in the Mahārājs' temples.**
  - Surat: Rs 1,00,000, 50,000 and 20,000 [S015].
  - Ahmedabad: Rs 80,000 [S013].
  - Kathiawar: "upwards of a score" of Gosāīnjī Mahārājs [S017].
  - In Ahmedabad the Vaiṣṇava head of the cloth guild stands against the Jain Nagar Seth [S014].
- **Followers are castes, not Brahman households alone.** Kathiawar's 23,000 Mochīs are "mostly followers of
  Svaminarayan" [S022]. Kāṭhīs, Kolis and Bhīls are converts [S019].

### Mādhva houses

- **Udupi and South Canara:**
  - Shivaḷḷi Brahmans "warmly adopted" Madhva after having been Smārtas [S031].
  - The eight maṭhs rotate the Kṛṣṇa temple every two years. Each svāmī spends fourteen years "levying
    contributions" in Canara and Mysore [S032–S033].
  - Konkaṇī Mādhvas keep their own maṭhs [S034].
  - Sode holds the Sonār goldsmiths, with power to excommunicate and fine [S035]. The Vyāsarāya maṭh at Anegundi
    holds the Gāṇigas [S036].
  - 1973: the same picture, with the Mādhva hold extended to Dharmasthala, a Śaiva temple whose priests are
    Mādhvas [S054–S060].
- **Sonda (Kanara pt 2):**
  - The Terbidi/Vādirāja maṭh is a branch of Udupi. It has land grants of 1593 and 1706, and pilgrims from Dharwar
    to Kumbakonam [S039].
  - "The Vaishnavs hold him in as much reverence as the Smarts hold Shankaracharya" [S040].
  - Its bell is a trophy over a Liṅgāyat priest [S041].
- **Dharwar 1884:**
  - The Uttarādi house, at Savanur, holds over two-thirds of the Vaiṣṇav Brahmans and the Rāghavendra house about
    a third [S062–S063].
  - 16 of 18 Mādhva sects accept the Uttarādi head. The Vyāsarāya and Rāghavendra lines are "on unfriendly terms"
    with him [S064].
- **Bellary:** the Brahmans are mostly Mādhva. Rāghavendra's tomb at Mantrālayam is endowed and draws pilgrims
  "including even Lingayats" [S162].

### Śrīvaiṣṇava houses

- **Vānamāmalai (Nāṅguneri), the Teṉkalai Jīyar's seat:**
  - It received the temple funds in 1841 and a Rs 8,694 dastik.
  - 200 subordinate maṭhs remit their surplus, and "All this wealth is at the absolute disposal of the Jeer"
    [S129–S130].
- **Teṉkalai–Vaḍakalai litigation:** Tiruvēndipuram (from 1760 to the Privy Council in 1906), Conjeeveram,
  Mannārguḍi and Tirukkuṟuṅguḍi [S131–S152].
- **Mysore:**
  - Melukote is the Śrīvaiṣṇava seat. It has the 1614 royal estate, 400 Brahmans on daily allowance, and the
    Parakāla svāmī [S048].
  - In the 1891 census the Śrīvaiṣṇavas are the largest Brahman sub-division in Mysore, Hassan and even Kadur
    (5,357 against 2,384 "Smārta") [S050–S052].
- Ahobila, Śrīperumbudūr, Tirukkōyilūr and Srirangam are **not** described: their districts are not in the set.

### Liṅgāyat maṭhs

- **The largest religious block of the Karnatak:**
  - Dharwar: at least 300,000 (38.5% of Hindus), with about 40,000 Jangams [S082].
  - Bijapur: about 220,000. Belgaum: about 237,000.
  - Bellary: 12% of the population, 7–8 times the Brahmans [S156].
- **Hierarchy:**
  - Murugha (Chitradurga) is the supreme guru.
  - Appeals go to the four or five siṃhāsanas (Ujjani, Bāḷehonnūr, Śrīśaila and others).
  - Below them are the virakta maṭhs (Hubli Mūrusāvira, Gadag/Dambal) and village hiremaṭhs [S141–S142].
- **Property:**
  - Hubli Mūrusāvira is the largest of Hubli's 15 maṭhs. It broke from Chitradurga about 1727 [S110].
  - Dambal has branch maṭhs "all endowed with lands".
  - Kaneri (Kolhapur): Rs 1,500 a year in 1886 and Rs 1,300 in 1959, with 5,000–6,000 disciples [S092, S115].
- **Pilgrim draw:**
  - Ulvi (Kanara): 10,000–12,000 pilgrims [S044].
  - Murugha's festival at Chitaldroog: 4,000 [S047].

### Tamil non-Brahmin Śaiva maṭhs (Tanjore 1906, Tinnevelly 1917)

These are the richest rival houses in the whole assignment.

- **Tiruvāvaḍuturai:** about 29,000 acres (25,000 in Tinnevelly); about Rs 2 lakhs a year; about 15 temples
  [S124].
- **Dharmapuram:** about 2,500 acres; 27 temples [S122].
- **Tiruppanandāḷ:** Rs 80,000 a year and a Benares branch. It was held independent of Dharmapuram in court
  [S120–S121].
- **Veḷḷāḷas** "compete closely with Brahmans" through these maṭhs, to which the gurukkaḷs of lower castes are
  subordinate [S133].

### East: Gauḍīya houses and Purī's maṭhs

- **Purī's maṭhs:**
  - In 1848, 29 maṭhs drew Rs 1,45,400 a year from land [S170]. Hunter puts their rental at £20,000 rising to
    £27,000 [S181].
  - The richest are Emār, Dakṣiṇapārśva and Rāghavadāsa. They are Rāmānandī in 1908 [S171–S178] and "Ramanuj" in
    1977 [S222].
  - Emār's mahant bought a four-anna share of the Kotdesh zamindari in 1896.
  - Uttarapārśva received 15 villages [S223].
- **The Gauḍīyas at Purī** have only two maṭhs in 1908 [S173]. In the 1977 list of about 130 maṭhs they have about
  30 [S218–S219]. From 1951–74 the maṭhs come under state endowment control [S220].
- **The Bengal Gosvāmī houses (Śāntipur, Khardah):**
  - They divide Bengal into circles with officers. The initiation fee is 20 annas, 16 of them to the Gosvāmī.
  - They inherit the property of heirless Bairāgīs.
  - In Bankura they levy Rs 1-6 on each initiation, marriage and death [S185–S192].
- **1872 headcounts:** Vaiṣṇavas run to tens of thousands per district against Sannyāsīs in tens or hundreds.
  Midnapur 96,178 against 436; Bardwan 37,362 against 84 [S202–S204].
- **Kenduli's Jayadeva fair:** over 50,000 pilgrims [S200].

## Comparison with Śaṅkara's following in the same places

### Wealth

Wherever both are described, the rival houses are as rich as the Śaṅkara seats or richer.

| place | rival house(s) | Śaṅkara seat |
|---|---|---|
| Tanjore | Tiruvāvaḍuturai, about Rs 2 lakhs a year | Kumbakoṇam Śaṅkara maṭh, about Rs 14,000 a year and three temples [S127] |
| Purī | the Vaiṣṇava maṭhs: villages and rupees | Govardhan maṭh: only a library and a place in the temple, no estate figure; about 7 Śaṅkara-line houses of about 130 in 1977 [S174, S221] |
| Bombay Karnatak | Kaneri, Rs 1,500 a year (the largest stated) | Saṅkeśvar, 30 inām villages and Rs 30,000 (`../../sringeri/README.md`) |

In the Bombay Karnatak only the Saṅkeśvar seat is given a full estate. There, the Liṅgāyat network is denser rather
than richer.

### Followings

The rival followings are larger, and they are not confined to Brahmans.

- **Gujarat:**
  - Against 647,256 Vallabhas and 287,687 Svāminārāyaṇs, the Dwarka Śāradā pīṭh is given no following. Its pontiff
    is a "referee" for Śaivas [S008].
  - In Ahmedabad city, Svāminārāyaṇ sādhus (315) outnumber Gosāīs and Vairāgīs (261). Brahmacārīs and Sannyāsīs
    number 49 [S012].
  - In Cutch and Baroda, however, the Gosāīs (Atīts) are the largest ascetic body: 4,877 of 6,840, and 10,014 of
    20,551. They hold the landed monasteries in Kathiawar [S018, S025–S026].
- **Canara:**
  - Śṛṅgeri keeps the non-Brahmin castes.
  - The Brahmans are mostly Mādhva: Sturrock says the Śṛṅgeri guru heads only "such of the Tulu Brahmins … as have
    remained Smarthas" [S030].
  - The Sonda Mādhva head's North Kanara jurisdiction is limited to Sonārs and Vaiṣṇav Deshasths [S040].
- **Mysore, Śṛṅgeri's own Kadur district:** Liṅgāyats are more than twice all Brahmans (37,657 against 17,026)
  [S052].

### Who follows instead of a Śaṅkara seat

- Liṅgāyat lay castes, Dhors and Liṅgāyat Mālīs: Chitradurga, Hubli or Kaneri.
- Konkaṇī Sonārs and Vāṇīs: Udupi, Sode, Vyāsarāya or Partagāḷī.
- Gāṇigas: Vyāsarāya [S036].
- Kanchugāras and Patvegārs: the Rāmacandrapura Smārta maṭh, a separate Smārta line [S037].
- Tamil Veḷḷāḷa and lower castes: the Śaiva ādhīnams.
- Bengali Chasi Kaibarttas, trading castes, Bāgdīs and Namaśūdras: the Gosvāmī houses.
- Gujarat artisans and Kolis: Svāminārāyaṇ.

### Direct conflicts with Śaṅkara seats

These are few:

- **The Kudalgi suit (Dharwar 1884, p. 757; S076).** About 1830 the Śaṅkara svāmī of Kudalgi (Kudli) sued the
  Hubli Mūrusāvira svāmī over a crosswise palanquin and lost at every level up to the Privy Council. The 1959
  revision drops the suit.
- **The Konkan Kuṇbīs, 1881 [S103].** Śṛṅgeri's agent won the 14,812 Konkan Kuṇbīs from the Chitradurga guru.
- **Chitaldroog [S046].** The Murugha guru cannot use his hill maṭh because of "the opposition of other sects".
- **Bellary [S164].** Kelsall only conjectures that Śṛṅgeri was "alarmed by the increasing numbers of the
  Jangams".
- **Bijapur [S090].** The old "keen rivalry" of Smārtas and Mādhvas "has to a great extent passed away".

The live disputes are within the rival traditions: Teṉkalai against Vaḍakalai, Dharmapuram against Tiruppanandāḷ,
Uttarādi against Rāghavendra, and Mūrusāvira against Chitradurga.

### Hooghly reverses the pattern

- The Guptipara maṭh has Caitanya and Nityānanda shrines and Rs 1,000 a month in endowed land. It was kept by
  Sarasvatī mahants [S210].
- By 1972 it is "run by the Dasanamis" under the Tārakeśvar mahant [S211].

## Limits

- **Missing districts:**
  - Trichinopoly (Srirangam), Kurnool (Ahobilam, Śrīśaila), Ramnad and North Arcot 1905 are not in the download
    set. So Ahobila, Śrīperumbudūr, Tirukkōyilūr and the Srirangam jīyars are undescribed.
  - No Uttarādi or Vyāsarāya maṭh is described in a Places entry. They appear only through followings and in the
    1959 Dharwar revision [S107–S109].
- **Headcounts:**
  - Few headcounts split Brahmans by sect. The Mādhva shares in Dharwar are within the Mādhvas only.
  - Rice's 1891 sub-divisions mix sect and regional labels: Badaganāḍ, Hala Karṇāṭaka and others are largely
    Smārta. So "Smārta" there understates Śaṅkara's Brahman following [S050–S053].
- **Tradition labels:**
  - Gujarat's "Rāmānuja" returns largely mean Rāmānandī-type Vaiṣṇavas.
  - Purī's Emār and Uttarapārśva change label between 1908 (Rāmānandī) and 1977 (Rāmānuja), and are coded as each
    source has them.
  - The 1977 Purī sect tallies are approximate: the OCR misaligned the list.
- **Gauḍīya places:** Navadvīp, Śrīkhaṇḍa and Bāghnāpāṛā have no institutional Places entry. The Gauḍīya picture
  comes from the caste chapters and Hunter.
- **Kathiawar and Cutch:** the Nāth (Kānphaṭā) rows [S018, S024] are included because these volumes were read
  here. They may overlap with the north assignment's coding.
- **Pages:**
  - Pages come from running heads and may be off by one; ranges mark page breaks.
  - Census paragraphs in Rice and the Baroda mendicant table are cited by entry or approximately.
  - The Svāminārāyaṇ total is read from the second scan of IX-1, the first being garbled.
- **Overlaps:** the 1872 Bengal Vaiṣṇava and Sannyāsī counts duplicate `../../bengal/results/gosain_counts.tsv`
  where the districts overlap.
