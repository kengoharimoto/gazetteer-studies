# Benares, Ayodhyā and Mathurā: Śaṅkara's following and its rivals

> **The study's verdict** (`../../SYNTHESIS.md`, "The answer"; `../README.md`): Śaṅkara's
> tradition was regional, not dominant. This report supplies part of the evidence for the "elsewhere".


Coded rows: `rivals/results/coded/BAM.tsv` (BAM01–BAM129). Every quote has been checked against the OCR, with
whitespace collapsed. The Daśanāmī Gosāins, Daṇḍīs and Nāgās are coded `tradition=other`, with `dasnami` in the
notes, so that each city has both sides in one file. Muslims and Jains are also coded `other`, with `muslim` or
`jain` in the notes.

### Benares

**Who dominated.** Every layer calls Benares the Śaiva city. Atkinson, following Siva Prasad, lists nineteen Śaiva
sub-sects, "all of which are generally found in Benares", and says Śiva's followers "occupy, it is said, nearly
one-half of the city" (p. 55). He adds that the Daṇḍīs "are chiefly found in Benares". Nevill qualifies this. He
calls Benares "nominally ... a great Saivite centre", where true Śaivites are probably under half. The 1901 return
shows only 4.3 % Śaivites against 4.8 % Vaiṣṇavites. He also says "Vedantism is the philosophy which almost
exclusively prevails" and that "the shrines dedicated to the cult of Shiva greatly predominate" (pp. 91–92).

**What each tradition held:**
- **Daśanāmī:**
  - The Gosāin caste was a wealthy, landed class. In 1881 there were 1,391 in the city, and Atkinson says they had
    "acquired considerable landed property".
  - Nevill (1909) gives them 10,304 acres, more than half of it in pargana Pandrah, where they held 18.3 %.
  - Goshain Narayangir's Surajkund math held a large Mirzapur estate and 437 acres in Benares (Rs 2,354).
  - A Daṇḍī satra was among the eleven richest charities, which together had Rs 6,690 a month.
  - Nāgā akhāṛās of the Nirbānī, Nirañjanī and Jūnā orders stood at Shivala and Hanuman ghats. The Jūnā was "fairly
    wealthy, owning some land". Nevill nevertheless says "The Nagas are not strong" here.
  - The religion chapter names Daśanāmīs only once: "Other sects comprise Dasnamis, Kunls and many who are little
    known".
- **Rāmānandī Bairāgīs:**
  - Bara Gudarji akhāṛā: about 30 Bairāgīs, roughly 300 years old, supported by the Maharaja of Rewah.
  - Chhoti Gudarji and the Digambari akhāṛā (10 Bairāgīs).
  - The Baid akhāṛā, with land at Khajuripur.
  - The Vishnupanthi akhāṛā, ascribed to Rāmānuja and said to be the oldest in Benares.
  - Atkinson records a feud: the Vaiṣṇava Nāgās "have a deadly feud with the Sannyāsis", and fights leave "several
    killed and wounded" (p. 52).
- **Vallabha:**
  - Atkinson says the "Gosain Gokul" disciples are almost only Gujarati Banias.
  - Nevill says "the power of Vallabhacharya is strongly felt". The Gopāl Mandir owns ten villages and two mahals
    (Rs 3,062).
  - RV21 records a Vallabha math at Jathanbar.
- **Gauḍīya:** only RV21 mentions them: a math Chaitanya "is said" to have founded. Nevill says the Bengali community
  is mainly Śaivite, "as the Vaishnavites more commonly resort to Muttra".
- **Kabīrpanthīs:**
  - Atkinson: 34 men in 1881; "many of them live" in Kabir Chaura.
  - Nevill: a large settlement in Kabirchaura of about 400 persons.
  - RV21: Kabirchaura and Kabir Sahib-ki-baithak are still revered.
- **Aghorīs:** Atkinson describes them. Nevill records the Kinaram akhāṛā (about 300 years old), whose Aughars "eat
  food touched by Hindus and Musalmans alike". RV21 has only a village shrine to Kina Ram.
- **Nāth Jogīs:** 159 men in 1881 and 419 persons in 1891. There were two Gorakhpanthi akhāṛās (Town Hall and Kal
  Bhairon) in 1909 and again in 1965.
- **Others:**
  - The Udāsī Panchaiti kalan akhāṛā had an income of about Rs 10,000 and seven branches. A separate Udāsī estate
    covered 1,046 acres.
  - The Jaṅgam (Liṅgāyat) Jangambari math held 1,054 acres (Rs 2,679).
  - The Theosophical Society tried to "combine the logical monism of Shankaracharya with the more human teaching of
    Ramanand".
  - There were no Āryas in 1891 and 176 in 1901.

**Across the three dates:**
- **Atkinson** gives a sect catalogue in which the Śaiva and Śaṅkara orders (Daṇḍī, Atīth/Gosāin/Sannyāsī,
  Paramhans) carry the most prestige, but almost no institutions or numbers.
- **Nevill** gives institutions and estates. The Daśanāmī estate (10,304 acres) is larger than any single Vaiṣṇava
  estate he names: the Gopāl Mandir's ten villages, or the Vaiṣṇava akhāṛās that live on charity and a few
  properties. Yet his religion chapter treats the Daśanāmīs as one sect among many.
- **RV21 (1965)** copies Nevill's akhāṛā list, with the Nāgā akhāṛās now "small". The Gosāin land is gone after
  zamindari abolition. A new Bhinga Daṇḍī Ashram (1924, Rs 8,000 a year) replaces the Daṇḍī satra. The 1809–10 Lāṭ
  Bhairon riot, in which Gosāins helped lead the Hindu mob, appears only in this layer.
- **The 1891 census** is the only district headcount. The Daśanāmīs (1,899), or Goshains (4,106), outnumber the
  Bairāgīs (141) and Jogīs (419).

### Ayodhyā (Fyzabad)

**Who dominated.** The Rāmānandī Bairāgī akhāṛās dominate in every layer.

**What each tradition held:**
- **The seven akhāṛās:** Nirbānī (at Hanumāngaṛhī), Nirmohī, Digambarī, Khākī, Mahānirbānī, Santokhī and Nirālambhī.
  - Oudh Gazetteer (1877): the Nirbānī mahant has 600 disciples, 300–400 of them resident. The Digambarī has 15. The
    Mahānirbānī has 25. The Nirmohīs hold rent-free land in Basti, Mankapur and Khurdabad.
  - Nevill (1905): not more than 250 resident Nirbānīs. They are "very wealthy", with revenue-free land in five
    districts, money-lending, elephant dealing and bought villages. The Digambarīs, though only 15, hold Gorakhpur
    lands and two villages. The Khākīs are given as "180 persons, of whom 650 are resident" (sic). The Santokhī akhāṛā
    was deserted after 1900. Other endowed houses are the Bara Asthan, Bhagwan Das-Achari (Basti villages) and
    Jankighat.
  - RV13 (1960): the akhāṛās "belong to the Ramanuj sect", in three anīs. Nirbānī residents number at most 200, and
    the Khākī akhāṛā has 32 groups.
- **Conflicts with Muslims:** the 1855 fight over the Janamasthān and Hanumāngaṛhī left 75 Muslims buried and 11 Hindus
  killed (1877, 1905). It was followed by Maulvi Amir Ali's march to destroy Hanumāngaṛhī (1905). The Nirmohīs had once
  held the Janamasthān.
- **Jains:** six temples, five Digambar and one Śvetāmbar. A Gaur Brahman tended them for a Lucknow Sarāwag community.
  There were 63 Jains in the district in 1901.
- **Kabīrpanthīs and Nāths:** nothing at Ayodhyā. There were 24 Jogīs in the district in 1891. Ten Aghorīs were counted
  in Ajodhya town in 1869.

**The Śaiva lay side.** The 1869 town table gives Ajodhya 2,075 Śaivas (Shaivi 975 + Shakti 1,100) against 2,222
Vaiṣṇavas. Fyzabad city had 14,620 Śaivas against 3,655 Vaiṣṇavas, and 25 Śiva temples against 10 Viṣṇu temples. The
Oudh Gazetteer notes that in the district the Vishnuite faith "is less popular than the Shakti or Shaivi".

**Across the three dates:**
- The Bairāgī institutions are nearly identical in 1877 and 1905; Nevill largely reworks the Oudh text.
- The changes are:
  - fewer resident Nirbānīs (300–400, then 250, then at most 200);
  - more Nirbānī business and land;
  - Ajodhya's population: 9,949 in 1869, 11,648 in 1881, 21,584 in 1901.
- RV13 adds the founding story: Gosāins, "followers of Shankaracharya" and "worshippers of Bhairav", held the
  Hanumāngaṛhī site and "were very inimical to the Vaishnavas". The akhāṛā chiefs "drove out these Gosains" (p. 88).
- **The census contradicts this picture.** In 1891 the district had **5,371 Daśanāmīs against 2,017 Bairāgīs**. Yet
  the gazetteers place no Daśanāmī house at Ayodhyā. Nevill's only trace is 1,847 Atīts in Tanda and Bikapur (1901),
  together with the Faqīrs, Goshains and Bairāgīs who between them owned 47 of 2,051 estates.

### Mathurā (Muttra, Brindaban, Gokul)

**Who dominated.** The Vaiṣṇava houses dominate at every date. Nevill: "It is usual to speak of Muttra as the
headquarters of Vaishnavism ... and Benares as the centre of Saivism."

**What each tradition held:**
- **Vallabha (Gokul):**
  - The seat has been at Gokul since 1565.
  - Atkinson: the head, Parshotam Lal, was the largest Gosāin zamindar, with seven or eight villages. 11,810 acres in
    tahsil Mat were dedicated to the Dwārkādhīś temple (the Seths' temple, whose Vallabha link is inferred).
  - Nevill: the head "once held a number of villages", and 32,344 adherents were returned in 1901.
- **Śrīvaiṣṇava:** the Rangjī temple was built for 45 lakhs (1845–51) by the Seth brothers, converts from Jainism.
  - It feeds 500, later 600, Śrīvaiṣṇavas daily.
  - It spent Rs 57,000 a year (Atkinson), later Rs 60,000 (Nevill).
  - 1,878 acres were dedicated to it, and its trustees are principal proprietors of Muttra tahsil and half of
    Radhakund.
  - Atkinson: it is "attended only by foreigners".
- **Gauḍīya:**
  - The old Brindaban temples include Gobind Deva, built in 1590 under Rūpa and Sanātana. The Gobind Deva Gosāin is
    "head of the Bengali Vaishnavas".
  - Pilgrims "from Lower Bengal" come to die at Brindaban.
  - Nevill: their numbers were "not separately recorded".
- **Rādhāvallabhī and Haridāsī:**
  - Atkinson calls the Rādhāvallabhīs "the most prominent" at Muttra. Nevill gives 18,827 in 1901, more than in any
    other district.
  - The Haridāsī Gosāins number about 500 and own the Bānke Bihārī temple, which was rebuilt for Rs 70,000.
- **Rāmānandīs:** 49,509 in 1901. Bairāgīs were 9,812 in 1881.
- **Gosāin landholding:** 9,109 acres in 1901, nearly all in Muttra tahsil. In Muttra the term means the Vaiṣṇava temple
  Gosāins.
- **Jains:** Seth Raghunath Das's temple. Conversions caused disturbances at Kosi, and the "naked gods" were held "in
  unaffected horror".
- **Ārya Samāj:** 1,018 members in 1901, 591 of them in Mat tahsil, mostly Jats.

**Across the dates.** Atkinson (1884) and Nevill (1911) tell the same story, Nevill with the 1901 sect figures added.
The figure that does not fit is the 1891 census: 17,131 Goshains, every one "unspecified", and zero Bairāgīs and zero
Jogīs. Atkinson counted 9,812 Bairāgīs + 3,627 Jogīs in 1881. Nevill counted 18,272 Faqīrs but only 803 Gosāins in
1901. The 1891 Muttra "Goshain" line therefore most likely holds the whole ascetic population of Braj. The census
README reads it as "the Vaiṣṇava lineages of Brindaban and Gokul"; that reading should be loosened. There is no
revised layer.

## Comparison with Śaṅkara's following in the same places

- **Benares** is the only one of the three cities where Śaṅkara's following is institutionally present in all
  layers:
  - Daṇḍīs ("chiefly found in Benares"; 150 Daṇḍī pandits helped Dārā Shikoh);
  - the Gosāin caste (1,391 in the city in 1881; 1,899 Daśanāmīs and 2,207 unspecified Goshains in the district in
    1891);
  - landed Gosāins (10,304 acres) and the Surajkund math;
  - three Nāgā akhāṛās, a Daṇḍī satra, and later the Bhinga Daṇḍī Ashram.

  Its rivals are institutionally many but small: Bairāgī akhāṛās of 10–30 men, Kabīrpanthīs (about 400), Nānakpanthīs
  (400–500), two Gorakhpanthi akhāṛās and Kinaram. Only the Gopāl Mandir (10 villages), the Udāsīs (1,046 acres; Rs
  10,000 income) and the Jaṅgams (1,054 acres) have estates comparable to the Gosāins'.

  The Śaṅkara side's weakness is in the texts' attention, not in its numbers. Nevill devotes paragraphs to Kabīrpanthīs,
  Nānakpanthīs and Rādhāsvāmīs and one clause to "Dasnamis". By 1965 the Gosāin estates have disappeared from view.
- **Ayodhyā** is a Bairāgī town in the texts. The district, however, holds one of the province's densest Daśanāmī
  populations: 5,371 in 1891, in a division with 27,844. In the 1869 town table Śaivas almost equal Vaiṣṇavas
  (2,075 / 2,222). The gazetteers give the Gosāins no house there. Their only appearances are as cavalry officers of
  Shuja-ud-daula (Himmat Bahadur, Umrao Gir), as Atīts in the outer tahsils, and, in RV13, as the earlier occupants
  of Hanumāngaṛhī expelled by the akhāṛās. That expulsion is the one explicit Daśanāmī–Bairāgī conflict in the three
  cities. In the Benares texts the parallel is Atkinson's generic Nāgā–Sannyāsī feud.
- **Mathurā** has effectively no Śaṅkara following in any layer:
  - Atkinson: "There are no available sources of special information regarding those [Śaiva sects] represented in this
    district";
  - 1891: zero Daśanāmīs;
  - 1901: Śaivites 1.5 %, "the majority of them ... Lingaits and Gorakhpanthis".

  The only Daśanāmī figure is the warlord Himmat Bahadur Gosāin (earlier coding). "Gosāin" at Mathurā means a
  Vaiṣṇava temple lineage. The Gosāins' 9,109 acres there belong to that sense, unlike Benares, where the same word
  covers a Daśanāmī landed caste.

In short, Śaṅkara's following was strongest at Benares and absent at Mathurā. At Ayodhyā it was numerous in the
district but written out of the city.

## Limits

- **No revised Mathura gazetteer:** the 1968 volume has not been downloaded, so Mathurā has two layers, not three.
- **No caste count for the Daśanāmīs after 1891:** the Nevill volumes give none (Benares none; Fyzabad only Atīts;
  Muttra 803 "Gosains" in the Vaiṣṇava sense), and the revised volumes none. The 1891 table is therefore the only
  comparable count. Its Muttra row is anomalous, and its Benares Bairāgī figure (141) is implausibly low beside the
  city akhāṛās.
- **The 1901 sect percentages are unreliable:** the sect returns (Śaivite 4.3 %, Vaiṣṇavite 4.8 %, Rāmānandī 5.5 %
  etc.) are, by the gazetteers' own account, vague. The 1869 Oudh town tables are partly "estimates based on ratios".
- **OCR problems:**
  - margin notes interleave the quotes (BAM06, BAM12, BAM85);
  - the RV21 figures are garbled (Muslims "1.9T890");
  - Nevill's Benares Jains in the municipality read "843" for 343;
  - Nevill's Khākī figures ("180 ... of whom 650 are resident") are internally inconsistent.
- **Inferred sect affiliations:**
  - Dwārkādhīś = Vallabha (BAM10);
  - Gopāl Mandir = Vallabha (from p. 92, BAM54);
  - Bhagwan Das-Achari = Rāmānuja-leaning (BAM106);
  - the Bengali pilgrims at Brindaban = Gauḍīya (BAM11).

  All are flagged in the notes.
- **An error in the earlier coding:** `north/results/coded/UP23.counts.tsv` reads the page number 241 as the
  Vishnupanthi akhāṛā's disciple count. Nevill gives no number for it.
- **Thinner coverage of district-wide material:** I read the city and religion sections fully, and other passages
  only through keyword hits. District-wide Gosāin/Bairāgī village holdings are coded only where the text sums them.

## Addendum: Mathura at the third date (revised gazetteer, 1968)

The revised Mathura volume (`dli.csl.3090`) was downloaded after this report was written and coded as BAM130–131.
It confirms the picture:
- Its religion section is Vaiṣṇava: the Vallabha, Haridāsī, Bengali/Gauḍīya and Rāmānandī sub-sects.
- Faqirs, "including the Bairagis, Gosains and Jogis", are numerous because Mathura is a religious centre.
- Dayānanda Sarasvatī's study at Mathura (the Ārya Samāj) is recalled.
- The only Śaṅkara trace is a monthly, *Sri Shankaracharya Updeshamrat* (1961), published from Mathura.
- No Daśanāmī house appears at any of the three dates.
